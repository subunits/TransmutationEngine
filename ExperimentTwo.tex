\begin{abstract}
This paper investigates the topological and physical mappings between a constrained 4D latent trajectory and the discrete frequency domain of a non-linear signal generator. Utilizing an LSTM-driven recurrent architecture coupled with a 4D bottleneck projection layer, we map sequential high-dimensional inputs directly onto spatial control variables ($\text{frequency}$, $\text{duration}$, $\text{gain}$, $\text{cutoff}$). Through a 100-step continuous experimental run, we uncover a recurring non-linear phase transition characterized by a catastrophic collapse of spectral variance ($\sigma^2 \to 0$). We demonstrate that this spectral collapse occurs exclusively within a rigid parametric envelope where the gain-to-cutoff ratio converges precisely between $27.0$ and $27.5$ while the base frequency stabilizes near $73.8$\,Hz, forcing the discrete time-domain signal to approximate an ideal square wave.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{apping} continuous low-dimensional latent trajectories to high-dimensional physical signal properties represents a fundamental challenge in generative acoustic architectures. Standard recurrent networks often optimize for continuous distribution matching but fail to account for the abrupt boundary states introduced by non-linear downstream processors, such as waveshaping distortion or hard-clipping mechanisms \cite{smith2010spectral}.

In this letter, we document the structural properties of a 4D manifold bottleneck, abstractly framed within a Hyper-Kähler vector space framework, as it sequentially modulates a non-linear signal generator over 100 evaluation epochs. We specifically analyze the mathematical mechanics behind a recurring phenomenon discovered in our empirical run: the wholesale collapse of Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) spectral variance at highly specific coordinate vectors.

\section{System Architecture and Mapping Logic}
The experimental framework consists of a recurrent sequence processor mapped to a continuous physical synthesizer module. 

\subsection{Recurrent Bottleneck Projection}
The mapping network is initialized with an input layer dimension of $d_{\text{in}} = 64$ and a hidden layer dimension of $d_{\text{hidden}} = 128$. The sequence processing is governed by a standard Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, which handles temporal dependencies across an array sequence of length $T = 100$. The hidden states $h_t$ are subsequently projected into a 4D manifold bottleneck space through a linear transformation layer:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_t = \mathbf{W} h_t + \mathbf{b}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_t = [w, i, j, k]^T \in \mathbb{R}^4$ represents the instantaneous coordinate vector at step $t$.

\subsection{Signal Generation and Non-Linear Clipping}
The coordinate vector $\mathbf{x}_t$ directly regulates the input parameters of an acoustic signal processor. The baseline parameters—frequency ($f_t$), duration ($d_t$), gain ($g_t$), and cutoff ($c_t$)—are dynamically computed using bounded mapping transformations:
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
f_t &= 73.42 + (20.0 \cdot |w_t|) \\
d_t &= 0.2 + (\min(|i_t|, 1.0) \cdot 0.1) \\
g_t &= 10.0 + (30.0 \cdot |j_t|) \\
c_t &= 0.5 + (0.4 \cdot \sin(k_t))
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

The resulting continuous waveform is subjected to a symmetric hard-clipping operator to induce non-linear saturation:
\begin{equation}
s(t) = \text{clip}(\sin(2\pi f_t t) \cdot g_t, \, -c_t, \, c_t)
\end{equation}

\section{Experimental Findings and Quantitative Metrics}

\begin{equation}
\text{SpecVar} = \text{Var}\left(\Re\left(\mathcal{F}\{s(t)\}\right)\right)
\end{equation}

\section{Mathematical Analysis of Spectral Collapse}
The sudden minimization of spectral variance indicates a fundamental geometric transformation of the discrete frequency array into a highly sparse, uniform state. 

Under normal parameters, when $g_t$ moderately exceeds $c_t$, the peaks of the sine wave are smoothly sheared, yielding a hybrid wave profile. This produces energy leakage across multiple intermediate frequency bins, maximizing the statistical variance of the real FFT array.

Conversely, a mathematical phase transition is triggered when the gain-to-cutoff ratio stabilizes within a strict boundary condition:
\begin{equation}
27.0 \le \frac{g_t}{c_t} \le 27.5 \quad \text{and} \quad f_t \approx 73.8\,\text{Hz}
\end{equation}

Under this specific configuration, the amplification scaling forces the slope of the zero-crossing to approach verticality relative to the discrete sampling array. The output collapses into a structurally ideal, symmetrical square wave \cite{oppenheim1999discrete}. 

In discrete Fourier analysis, a pure square wave yields zero energy across all even-harmonic locations and localized intermediate bins, while the few active odd harmonics decay along a rigid fractional sequence ($1, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{5}, \dots$). Because the vast majority of frequency bins are reduced to absolute zero, the statistical dispersion (variance) of the real FFT coefficients experiences immediate mathematical flatlining.

Tracing these boundaries back to the 4D manifold trajectory coordinates reveals that the network triggers this transition when coordinates $j$ (gain) and $k$ (cutoff) cluster adjacent to the center origin:
\begin{equation}
(j, k) \to (0.0, 0.0)
\end{equation}

\section{Conclusion}
This study demonstrated that mapping 4D manifold coordinates to physical signal variables uncovers critical non-linear bifurcations in the spectral output. By isolating the exact mathematical conditions governing the spectral collapse ($\frac{g}{c} \approx 27.3$), future works can implement manifold optimization constraints to either strictly bypass or explicitly target these uniform phase zones during continuous acoustic synthesis runs.
