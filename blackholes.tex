\begin{abstract}
This paper outlines the geometric mapping of the Schwarzschild radius ($r_s$) into the complex structure of \kahler manifolds. We focus on the functional transition from Lorentzian spacetime to Euclidean Riemannian space and the resulting constraints on the \kahler potential.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Schwarzschild Metric}
The Schwarzschild metric is the unique vacuum solution for a static, spherically symmetric mass $M$. The line element is:
\begin{equation}
    ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right) c^2 dt^2 + \left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)^{-1} dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2
\end{equation}
where $r_s = 2GM/c^2$. The function $f(r) = (1 - r_s/r)$ determines the causal structure of the manifold.

\section{\kahler Mapping via Wick Rotation}
To interpret the Schwarzschild geometry as a \kahler manifold, we apply a Wick rotation $t \to i\tau$. This transforms the metric into a Euclidean signature. To avoid a conical singularity at $r = r_s$, the coordinate $\tau$ must be periodic with $\beta = 4\pi r_s$. 



This geometry is often referred to as the \textit{Schwarzschild-K{\"a}hler cigar}. On such a manifold, the metric is generated by a \kahler potential $\mathcal{K}$:
\begin{equation}
    g_{i\bar{j}} = \frac{\partial^2 \mathcal{K}}{\partial z^i \partial \bar{z}^j}
\end{equation}
The \kahler-Einstein condition $R_{i\bar{j}} = \Lambda g_{i\bar{j}}$ requires that the potential satisfies the complex Monge-Amp{\`e}re equation. For a vacuum black hole ($\Lambda = 0$), the manifold is Ricci-flat, placing strict requirements on the behavior of $\mathcal{K}$ as $r \to r_s$.
