\section{The Schwarzschild Geometry}
The Schwarzschild metric is the unique vacuum solution for a static, spherically symmetric system. In standard coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \phi)$, the line element is given by:
\begin{equation}
    ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{\rs}{r}\right) c^2 dt^2 + \left(1 - \frac{\rs}{r}\right)^{-1} dr^2 + r^2 (d\theta^2 + \sin^2 \theta d\phi^2)
\end{equation}
The \textbf{Schwarzschild radius}, defined as $\rs = \frac{2GM}{c^2}$, represents the event horizon. 

\subsection{Kruskal-Szekeres Completion}
To analyze the manifold beyond the coordinate singularity at $r = \rs$, we define the Kruskal coordinates $(U, V)$. The metric becomes:
\begin{equation}
    ds^2 = \frac{32(GM)^3}{r} e^{-r/2GM} (-dV^2 + dU^2) + r^2 d\Omega^2
\end{equation}
This confirms that the manifold is smooth at the horizon, a property that must be preserved when mapping to \kahler structures.



\section{\kahler Manifolds and Wick Rotation}
To connect with \kahler geometry, we perform a Wick rotation $t \to i\tau$. The Euclidean Schwarzschild metric near $r = \rs$ takes the form:
\begin{equation}
    ds_E^2 \approx \left( \frac{r-\rs}{\rs} \right) d\tau^2 + \left( \frac{\rs}{r-\rs} \right) dr^2 + \rs^2 d\Omega^2
\end{equation}
Setting $\rho^2 = 4\rs(r-\rs)$, the $(r, \tau)$ part of the metric becomes $\rho^2 d(\frac{\tau}{2\rs})^2 + d\rho^2$, which is the metric of a plane in polar coordinates if $\tau$ has period $8\pi GM$.

\subsection{The \kahler Potential}
On a \kahler manifold, the metric $g_{i\bar{j}}$ is derived from a local scalar function $\K(z, \bar{z})$:
\begin{equation}
    g_{i\bar{j}} = \partial_i \bar{\partial}_j \K
\end{equation}
For Ricci-flat solutions (Einstein manifolds with $\Lambda = 0$), the potential must satisfy the complex Monge-Amp{\`e}re equation:
\begin{equation}
    \det (\partial_i \bar{\partial}_j \K) = 1
\end{equation}
