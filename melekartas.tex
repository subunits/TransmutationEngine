\section{System Overview}
The Melakarta system represents a complete mathematical classification of the seventy-two fundamental parent scales in Carnatic music[cite: 4]. The framework is constructed through the systematic permutation of twelve semitones within a heptatonic structure[cite: 4]. This structure ensures that every possible melodic combination is accounted for within the parent scale scheme[cite: 4].

\section{Mathematical Structure}

\subsection{Madhyama Division}
The primary split divides the seventy-two scales into two groups of thirty-six based on the fourth note[cite: 4]. The first thirty-six scales use the lower frequency variation[cite: 4]. The latter thirty-six scales use the higher frequency variation[cite: 4].

\subsection{Chakra and Swara Permutations}
The system organizes these scales into twelve clusters known as chakras[cite: 4]. Within each chakra, the first three notes are held constant[cite: 4]. This provides a stable foundation for the lower portion of the scale[cite: 4]. To define the six individual scales within a chakra, the combinations of the sixth and seventh notes are rotated through a fixed repeating cycle[cite: 4].

\section*{Historical Foundation}
This logical framework was established by historical scholars[cite: 4]. Most notably, Venkatamakhin and Govindacharya defined the naming conventions and rules for these parent ragas[cite: 4].
