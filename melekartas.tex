\section{System Overview}
The Melakarta system is a complete mathematical classification of the seventy-two fundamental parent scales in Carnatic music[cite: 4]. This framework is constructed through the systematic permutation of twelve semitones within a heptatonic structure to ensure every possible melodic combination is accounted for[cite: 4].

\section{Mathematical Structure}

\subsection{Madhyama Division}
The system divides the seventy-two scales into two groups based on the fourth note[cite: 4]. The first thirty-six scales use the lower frequency variation ($M_1$), and the latter thirty-six use the higher frequency variation ($M_2$)[cite: 4].

\subsection{Chakra and Swara Permutations}
The scales are organized into twelve clusters known as chakras[cite: 4]. Within each chakra, the first three notes are held constant to provide a stable foundation[cite: 4]. The identity of each individual scale within a chakra is determined by rotating the combinations of the sixth and seventh notes through a fixed repeating cycle[cite: 4].

\section*{Historical Foundation}
This logical framework was established by historical scholars such as Venkatamakhin and Govindacharya, who defined the rules for these parent ragas[cite: 4].
